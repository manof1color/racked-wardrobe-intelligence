## Phase 24 â€” Garment cropping that follows the piece (2026-08-29)

- [#95](https://github.com/manof1color/racked-wardrobe-intelligence/pull/95) fixes the reported defect that garment pieces were not being cropped correctly. Two causes were measured rather than guessed. The three-view display crop relied on sharp's border trim, which needs a near-uniform frame edge and therefore failed on carpet, wooden flooring, and one-window lighting â€” the backdrops people actually photograph against. The look-scan cutout removed the backdrop well but then took bounds over every remaining opaque pixel, so a pillow beside the garment or the neighbours on a crowded rail dragged the crop wide. A new silhouette pass removes the backdrop and then keeps only the largest connected region, so the crop follows the piece instead of the frame's leftovers. `scripts/crop-benchmark.ts` scores every approach by intersection-over-union against a known garment rectangle across 14 seeded backdrops and is reproducible on any machine: border trim 61% mean IoU (7/14 usable), previous cutout 78% (10/14), silhouette pass 85% (12/14), with the decisive gains on a second object in shot (44% to 97%) and a crowded rail (37% to 97%). Both intake paths now share the pass, each keeping its existing fallbacks, and an ambiguous photo is declined rather than cropped to a guess. Writing the tests first surfaced two real defects in the new code â€” sharp orders `joinChannel` after `extract`, which silently discarded both the transparency and the crop â€” and a strongly patterned backdrop remains a recorded limitation rather than a fixed case.

## Phase 26 â€” Recognition that does not silently discard the garment (2026-08-30)

- [#97](https://github.com/manof1color/racked-wardrobe-intelligence/pull/97) fixes a reported failure where a single pair of shorts, photographed alone and clearly visible, came back as "No distinct clothing pieces were detected". The cause was not the model's vision but Racked's own parsing: the detection prompt asked for 0-1 fractional bounding boxes and the parser accepted nothing else, while an unreadable box discarded the entire detection. A probe of the seven conventions vision models actually emit showed six of them â€” percentages, source pixels, corner arrays, left/top/right/bottom keys, and a missing or null box â€” being silently dropped even when the garment was correctly identified, named, and classified. Generic category words such as "clothing" or "apparel" deleted the piece as well. Bounding-box reading now lives in `lib/detection-bounds.ts`, accepts every convention, rescales percentages and pixels using the real image dimensions, and falls back to the whole frame rather than discarding a garment the model named; an unrecognized category survives as an explicitly unknown piece for the person to correct. When detection genuinely returns nothing, the route no longer dead-ends with an HTTP 422 blaming the photograph: it offers the photo as one manual-review candidate with zero confidence and no invented attributes, which the intake UI labels "needs your label". The prompt now states its coordinate convention explicitly and tells the model that a photo of exactly one garment is normal. A separate latent defect surfaced during the work: `AI_HANGER_MODEL` was read in code but missing from the Amplify runtime allowlist, so setting it in production had always been silently ignored; it and the new `AI_LOOK_DETECTION_MODEL` are now registered, and a test fails if any `AI_*` override is ever read without reaching production. Ten tests were added, and the two guards were each confirmed non-vacuous by reintroducing the defect they cover.

## Phase 44 — Linking a brand product without its label, and enrolling one from a photo (2026-09-18)
