# TikTok campaign — Racked

Written 2026-09-16. Racked is a wardrobe app, not a clothing label, and the campaign has to
respect that difference from the first video.

## What we take from Children of Khan, and what we can't

[Children of Khan](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/children-of-khan) is a creator-led line
(Luca Maxim with Elite Ball Knowledge) selling through TikTok Shop, with live reviews doing
the selling.

**Transferable:** a named human fronting the brand, a stated ethos people want to be part of,
live and reply-driven formats, and selling inside the app people are already in.

**Not transferable:** they have a product to unbox. We have no haul, no drop, no restock.
The demo is somebody's own wardrobe, which is why closet audits — not product shots — are
our version of the unboxing video.

The wardrobe-app category already has its own TikTok genre: comparison videos (Whering vs
Acloset vs Indyx), closet-setup tutorials, and "influencer-favourite app" framing. That
genre exists whether or not we join it. We should be in it.

## Position on one line

**Shop your own closet.** The number that carries it is cost per wear. The angle nobody else
in the category can claim honestly: your closet stays private — brands only ever see
anonymous totals, and only above 25 owners.

## Five content pillars

1. **Closet audits.** "I scanned 41 pieces. Twelve have never been worn." Numbers on screen.
2. **Cost per wear.** The £/$ maths on one garment. The most re-watchable format we have.
3. **Get dressed with me**, with Hanger picking from what is already owned.
4. **One piece, five ways.** Rewear as the flex, against haul culture.
5. **Build in public.** Founder POV: the competition, the App Store, features cut to ship.

A sixth, for B2B recruiting, at most once a week: **what brands learn after checkout** —
the wear data that purchase history cannot show.

## Format rules

- Hook in the first 3 seconds, visual and verbal. Completion and rewatch are what the
  algorithm rewards, and the hook buys both.
- 15–34 seconds. Numbered series so viewers come back for the next one.
- 4–6 posts a week. Gaps beyond 5–7 days suppress reach; below 4 a week growth flattens.
- Batch on weekends, post on weekdays.
- Reply to comments with videos — the cheapest reliable series engine there is.

## Creators

Micro-creators at 10k–100k followers return several times the engagement of mega deals at a
fraction of the cost. Start with **10 creators × 3 videos**, on these terms:

- No scripts. One requirement: show their real closet.
- One mandated hook per brief, their words after that.
- Usage rights for Spark Ads written into the brief.
- `#ad` disclosure, every time, no exceptions.

## Paid

Spend nothing on cold creative. Amplify only what organic has already proven, through Spark
Ads, then add app-install campaigns once the store listings are live.

| Stage | Monthly | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Test | $0 | Organic only; find the two formats that work |
| Amplify | ~$500 | Spark Ads behind the top 2–3 organic posts |
| Acquire | ~$2,000 | App installs, measured on cost per install and D7 retention |

## Eight weeks

| Weeks | Focus | Ties to the build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Account live, 12 posts, founder series starts | Recruit closed testers from comments — **this is also the 12 testers Google Play requires** |
| 3–4 | Creator seeding, waitlist, TestFlight invitations | The 14-day Play tester clock runs here |
| 5 | Android launch week: 2 posts/day, reply videos | Play production access |
| 6–8 | Spark Ads on proven posts, comparison content, brand-side POV | iOS submission and review |

Recruiting testers through TikTok is the one place where the marketing plan and the store
plan pay for each other. Do it first.

## What we measure

Watch-through, rewatch and saves before views. Then installs, **scan completion rate** (did a
new person actually add a piece?), D7 retention, and cost per install. Review weekly; kill any
pillar that hasn't earned its slot after three weeks.

## Lines we don't cross

- No claim the product doesn't support: no photorealistic try-on, no fit or size prediction,
  no sales-lift or purchase-intent claims.
- No implying brand partnerships that don't exist.
- `DEMO` synthetic data never appears in marketing as if it were real users.
- Nobody else's face or closet without their consent.
- Disclose every paid partnership.
